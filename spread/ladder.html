What v1 and v2 are testing

The entry bar (the v1/v2 axis): a spread model's whole claim is that it disagrees with the number. How MUCH must it disagree before that's signal and not noise? v1 takes any pick a full point off the line; v2 waits for a league-sized margin (~2.5 pts) and won't touch a team without enough games played. A stricter bar bets less and should — if it's right — beat the close more often per bet. That's the one thing racing v1 against v2 measures, because they are otherwise the same bot.
The rungs (shared by both, the ablation axis): the model reads season-average scoring rates, and a trade doesn't move a season average by a single point — so the day after a team ships its leading scorer the model's number is unchanged while the market reprices in minutes. Each rung is one reaction to that:
  • Ceiling — reject any edge above +10%. A season scoring average does not find a 15% edge against a priced spread; that is the model being wrong, not the market.
  • Form — recency-weighted scoring rates blended 50/50 with the season rate. Adapts to a changed roster in weeks instead of never. Shrink is identical to the control's, so only the rate moves.
  • Clean — no bet when either side traded/released inside 3 days or has 3+ fresh significant injuries.
  • Sharp — quarter-Kelly staking instead of flat 2%.
Honest note: sigma, home-field edge, the form blend and the lookback windows are standard public estimates, not fitted. This ladder is what judges them. Don't cite them as tuned.
