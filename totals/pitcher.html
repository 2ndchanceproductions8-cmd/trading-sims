Playbook

The starting-pitcher rung. The Careful book with exactly one change: in MLB the expected total is adjusted for the two starting pitchers. Every other gate is literally Careful's, and outside MLB (or with a starter unlisted) the adjustment is zero and this book is bit-for-bit the control. Why: across 282 settled bets the correlation between this fleet's model edge and the actual result was −0.047 — zero, with the most confident bets doing worst. That is a model with no information, and no gate or stake change repairs it; the input must change. This bot reads season-average scoring rates, so the day a team starts its ace the model's expected total is identical to the day it starts a replacement — while the market repriced hours ago. Each starter's ERA is shrunk toward the day's own slate average by his decisions, scaled by the share of the game he throws, and both starters count: two aces pull the total down, two poor arms push it up. It may still not beat the close, which is why it is a parallel book judged on CLV rather than an edit to the control.

How the model reads a total

Each team's scoring rate and rate-allowed come from ESPN's standings, then get regressed toward the league average (a hot week isn't a rate). Offense then meets defense: exp_home = home_off × away_def / lg_avg, same for the away side, added together. A normal curve around that expected total — with a per-sport spread — gives P(over), which is compared against the over/under price with the vig stripped out.
The known blind spot: season averages don't know who is pitching tonight. In MLB the starting pitcher moves a run total more than either team's average does, and the market prices him — this model can't see him. Expect MLB to be the weakest league on this board. That's the experiment, not a bug.
