The humility ceiling. Careful with exactly one change: an edge above +10% is refused rather than backed. Why: the control bets almost entirely in its worst-calibrated regime — all 24 bets of the last race carried an edge over +11%, because three picks a day off a 15–19 game board always means the three most extreme disagreements. Fleet-wide, the 10%+ bucket returned −0.091 R, the worst there is, and this bot's own edge_ceiling is one of only two gate results it has ever produced that are robustly significant (−0.251, t = −2.62). Honest limit: this is gate-tuning, and gate-tuning cannot repair a signal that measures zero — across 282 settled bets the correlation between edge and result was −0.047. It bounds the damage from a bad model; it does not make the model good.

How the model reads a total

Each team's scoring rate and rate-allowed come from ESPN's standings, then get regressed toward the league average (a hot week isn't a rate). Offense then meets defense: exp_home = home_off × away_def / lg_avg, same for the away side, added together. A normal curve around that expected total — with a per-sport spread — gives P(over), which is compared against the over/under price with the vig stripped out.
The known blind spot: season averages don't know who is pitching tonight. In MLB the starting pitcher moves a run total more than either team's average does, and the market prices him — this model can't see him. Expect MLB to be the weakest league on this board. That's the experiment, not a bug.
