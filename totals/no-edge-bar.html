Playbook

The edge-bar ablation. This is the Careful book with exactly one change: the +4% edge bar drops to 0%, so the model only has to lean the right way. Every other gate is literally Careful's — both books call the same gate function with a different bar, so they cannot drift apart on anything else. Why it exists: the warehouse scored 380 picks Careful refused for being under the bar at +0.086 R — better than anything it actually bet. That reads like "the bar is too high", but it is t = 1.84 (not significant), it is one of ~22 comparisons run fleet-wide, and the refusals show no gradient: the picks just under the bar were flat (+0.035 R) while the strongest bucket was the one where the model actively disagreed. So the bar was not quietly lowered on a hunch — it is being raced, forward, against an untouched control. If the bar is dead weight, this bankroll will say so.

How the model reads a total

Each team's scoring rate and rate-allowed come from ESPN's standings, then get regressed toward the league average (a hot week isn't a rate). Offense then meets defense: exp_home = home_off × away_def / lg_avg, same for the away side, added together. A normal curve around that expected total — with a per-sport spread — gives P(over), which is compared against the over/under price with the vig stripped out.
The known blind spot: season averages don't know who is pitching tonight. In MLB the starting pitcher moves a run total more than either team's average does, and the market prices him — this model can't see him. Expect MLB to be the weakest league on this board. That's the experiment, not a bug.
