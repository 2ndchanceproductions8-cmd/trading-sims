The league-level rung. Careful with exactly one change: the league average is measured over the last 21 days of finished games instead of the whole season, and every team rate is re-based to that level. Relative team strength is untouched; only the level moves. Why: the league average is both the normaliser and the shrink target, so a stale one biases every game in the league at once. Measured on real WNBA finals — May 169.7 → Jun 175.3 → Jul 178.0 → Aug 184.3, last three weeks 185.0 — against a standings baseline of 175.07. Roughly ten points low, every game, all season, which is why the model showed value on the UNDER 25 times against the OVER 7 and the fleet duly bet WNBA unders 19 times for 8–11. Near-inert in a league that isn't drifting (MLB 8.97 season vs 8.74 recent), and if the window is too thin to trust, this book is the control.

How the model reads a total

Each team's scoring rate and rate-allowed come from ESPN's standings, then get regressed toward the league average (a hot week isn't a rate). Offense then meets defense: exp_home = home_off × away_def / lg_avg, same for the away side, added together. A normal curve around that expected total — with a per-sport spread — gives P(over), which is compared against the over/under price with the vig stripped out.
The known blind spot: season averages don't know who is pitching tonight. In MLB the starting pitcher moves a run total more than either team's average does, and the market prices him — this model can't see him. Expect MLB to be the weakest league on this board. That's the experiment, not a bug.
