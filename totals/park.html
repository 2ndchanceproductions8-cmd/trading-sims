Playbook

The park rung. Careful with exactly one change: each team's scoring rate has the parks it has played in divided back out, and tonight's park applied instead. Factors are derived in-house from finished games and shrunk toward 1.0. Why: across 1,239 MLB finals the spread runs Coors 1.179 (10.6 runs) to T-Mobile 0.894 (8.0) — 2.55 runs end to end, and one standard deviation is 0.60 runs, exactly the margin this bot needs before it calls a disagreement a bet. The control reverted every game toward the league average and therefore bet OVER in pitchers' parks (model 8.84, actual 8.15) and UNDER in hitters' parks (model 9.62, actual 10.09) — wrong in the direction it bet, both tails. The obvious version of this fix does nothing: simply multiplying by tonight's factor moved mean error 3.795 → 3.764, because a season rate already contains half a team's own park. Neutralising first is the entire point.

How the model reads a total

Each team's scoring rate and rate-allowed come from ESPN's standings, then get regressed toward the league average (a hot week isn't a rate). Offense then meets defense: exp_home = home_off × away_def / lg_avg, same for the away side, added together. A normal curve around that expected total — with a per-sport spread — gives P(over), which is compared against the over/under price with the vig stripped out.
The known blind spot: season averages don't know who is pitching tonight. In MLB the starting pitcher moves a run total more than either team's average does, and the market prices him — this model can't see him. Expect MLB to be the weakest league on this board. That's the experiment, not a bug.
